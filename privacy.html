Carioca Chile — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 October 2026

Carioca Chile (the “App”) is an iOS card-game app developed by Pascal Peltriaux. This policy explains what data the App handles and how. The short version: no accounts, no ads, no trackers, no third-party analytics.

What we don't collect

  • No email address, phone number, or real name.
  • No advertising identifier (IDFA) and no third-party analytics or advertising SDKs.
  • No location data.
  • No access to your device's photo library, contacts, microphone, or camera. (If you have a Game Center profile photo, the App may show it to the other players in a multiplayer game — see “Game Center” below.)
  • No payment information. The Unlock Full Game option is a one-time, non-consumable purchase processed entirely by Apple — we never see card numbers or billing details.

What stays on your device (and optionally syncs to your other Apple devices)

The following is stored locally in the App's sandbox on your device:

  • Your gameplay statistics (wins, streaks, round times).
  • Your settings (theme, language, AI difficulty, house rules, sound and haptics, the display name you choose).
  • The game in progress for solo, Quick Play and Pass & Play games, so you can resume after closing the App.
  • Your gems, the card finishes you unlocked, daily missions and season progress.
  • Your Daily and Weekly Challenge results and daily streak.
  • The list of duels you created or answered.

If you are signed into iCloud and the “iCloud sync” toggle is on (it is by default), most of this data is also mirrored to Apple's iCloud Key-Value Store so it stays consistent across your Apple devices using the same Apple ID. It lives in your personal iCloud account managed by Apple — the developer does not receive, see, or store it, and it is not sent to any third party. You can turn iCloud sync off at any time in Options; your existing iCloud copy stays in place so turning it back on restores it.

Deleting the App deletes the local copy. Resetting statistics from the in-app stats screen also clears the iCloud copy. To wipe all of the App's iCloud data without the in-app controls, remove it from Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage.

Device sensors

  • Motion. On the home screen the fan of cards tilts slightly as you move your phone. The App reads the motion sensor only while that screen is visible; the readings are used on the spot and are never stored or sent anywhere. Turning on iOS Reduce Motion switches this off.
  • iPhone Duo. On a foldable iPhone the App reads whether the phone is open, half-folded or closed, and where the fold is, so it can lay out the table around it. This is used only on your device and is never stored or sent anywhere.

Game Center

The App uses Apple Game Center for leaderboards, achievements and opponents' profile photos in multiplayer. Game Center is opt-in at the iOS level: if you are not signed in, none of the following happens.

  • Sign-in. On launch the App calls Apple's standard GKLocalPlayer.authenticate API. Apple returns your Game Center display name and player ID, which the App uses to name you in multiplayer games and to send achievement and score reports.
  • Profile photo. If you have a Game Center photo, the App fetches it with GKLocalPlayer.loadPhoto. In a multiplayer game it is included in the game's data on Firebase so the other players see it next to your name. It is deleted with the game (see “What gets sent when you play online”).
  • Achievements and leaderboards. When you finish a game or a Daily or Weekly Challenge, the App reports your achievements and scores to Apple so they appear on Game Center. This stays entirely within Apple's services and is governed by Apple's privacy policy.

To opt out, sign out of Game Center (Settings → Game Center → Sign Out) or remove your Game Center photo.

What gets sent when you play online

Online multiplayer uses Google Firebase Realtime Database. When you play an online game, the following is sent to Firebase and shared with the other players in that game:

  • The game's room code (a short word combination such as “brave-otter-4729”).
  • The display name you choose (any nickname — we never ask for a real name).
  • Your Game Center photo, if you have one, and the avatar frame you have equipped.
  • An anonymous Firebase Authentication identifier, used only to enforce access rules on the database. It is not linked to your Apple ID, your Game Center identity, or any other personal information.
  • The state of the game (cards in play, scores, whose turn it is) and any quick reactions (emoji) you send.

This is used only to keep the game in sync between the players' devices. It is deleted when the game ends; games left inactive for more than 24 hours are removed automatically.

The online queue. When you tap Play online and wait for an opponent, a short entry with your display name, the room code and the time is shown to other players of the App so they can join you. It is removed when the game starts, or within two minutes of you leaving.

“Alert me when a table opens.” If you choose this, your display name and an expiry time (15, 30 or 60 minutes, your choice) are added to a waiting list that other players of the App can see, so someone can open a table for you. Your entry is removed when you join a game, when you cancel, or when the time runs out.

Duels

When you create a duel, your display name, your score and the number that recreates the same deal are stored on Firebase under a random duel ID, together with your anonymous identifier. Anyone you send the duel link to can see them and answer once; their display name and score are added to the duel. Duels are deleted automatically 7 days after they are created.

Nearby play (local network)

“Host Nearby Game” and “Join Nearby Game” use Apple's MultipeerConnectivity over local Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. Your display name, avatar frame and the game state go directly to the other devices at the table — nothing goes through the internet or our servers. iOS asks for Local Network permission the first time; you can change it in Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network.

Push notifications (opt-in)

If you allow notifications and the in-app “Multiplayer hints” option is on, the App may send you:

  • a hint when someone is waiting for an online game;
  • a notice when someone answers a duel you created;
  • reminders when your daily streak is about to end, when unclaimed season rewards are about to expire, or when you haven't played this week's challenge.

One notification is sent even when Multiplayer hints is off, because you ask for it directly: “your game is ready”, after you choose “Alert me when a table opens”.

To deliver them, the App stores a device-specific Firebase Cloud Messaging token with your anonymous identifier, plus the little status needed to decide whether a reminder applies: the date of your last Daily Challenge and your streak length, the current season and how many rewards are waiting, and whether this week's challenge is done. The token is removed automatically if it stops working (for example after you uninstall the App), and hints sent to all players are rate-limited. You can turn notifications off in Options or in iOS Settings → Notifications → Carioca Chile.

Anonymous usage counts

Our server counts how many online games start each day, split into public and private games and by how many people played. These counts contain no names, identifiers, or device information and cannot be linked to you.

Third parties

The App relies on two platforms:

  • Apple — Game Center, iCloud, In-App Purchases and push delivery, under Apple's privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase — Realtime Database for online games, the queue and duels; Cloud Messaging and Cloud Functions for notifications and the daily game counts. Covered by Google's privacy documentation.

We use no other third-party services — no analytics SDKs, no ad networks, no crash reporting beyond Apple's built-in opt-in diagnostics.

Children's privacy

The App is rated 4+ and intended to be safe for children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. The display name used in multiplayer and duels can be any nickname — we encourage children and their parents to choose one that doesn't reveal a real identity.

Your rights

We don't keep any account data, so there is nothing to request, correct or delete on a server about you. To wipe all local data, delete the App. Online games are removed within 24 hours and duels after 7 days; if you want one deleted sooner, write to us at the address below.
